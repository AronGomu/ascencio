# World rules

> Status: working draft — Chapter 1 in design

## Fundamental axiom

A **god** rules this world and imposes its rules by its will alone. It makes the use of physical violence impossible as a means of acquisition.

> **Every conflict is necessarily resolved by play.** (*No Game No Life* logic)

**Definition of a conflict:** two parties claim ownership or exclusive use of the same good, without being able to exercise it simultaneously. Put otherwise: *two wills carry incompatible claims on the same value*. Incompatibility is the criterion — if both claims can be satisfied at the same time, there is nothing to play for.

**The god stays in the background / in lore** through the early chapters. It defines the frame of the possible; it does not intervene.

## Why Duel Monsters

A **spirit world** exists. Some spirits imprint their energy into manufactured cards — a discovery made empirically over successive print runs.<sup>[1]</sup> Duel Monsters is therefore the game best suited to resolving conflicts, and the one that prevailed.

Not every card is imprinted → real scarcity → a market → covetousness.

## Duel rules

- **A duel engaged over a conflict cannot be refused.** Refusing = losing automatically = ceding the stake.
- **The multi format is allowed**: several duelists may jointly engage a duel against a single person.
- **The rules do not balance that imbalance.** This is how power relations express themselves in this world.
- **Absolute prohibition:** a person's physical integrity may never be staked.
- **Sole exception:** Shadow Games, where the stake is the soul.

> **Acknowledged flaw in the system:** the god banned violence but let coercion by numbers through. A gang that comes five against one is not cheating — it is legally exploiting the frame. Underlying question to raise in the later chapters: is this world truly *just*, or merely *pacified*?

## Cheating

- Cheating is **not forbidden** by the rules.
- Cheating is sanctioned only if it is **discovered and demonstrated**.
- Mechanism: if a player establishes by a binding logical argument that the other is cheating, the cheater **automatically loses** the duel, and therefore the stake.
- **The accusation triggers the sanction only if it is true and proven.** If the proof does not hold, nothing happens: the accused does not lose, the duel continues.
- No formal counter-sanction for the accuser.

**Design consequence:** the duel is an **epistemological** confrontation as much as a technical one. Winning can mean observing, deducing, demonstrating. The world literally punishes whoever does not think well enough to catch the cheater.

**Implicit cost of a failed accusation:** time lost, information revealed to the opponent (you show what you observed and suspected), reputation.

## Social status of duelists

Duelists are literally stronger than other people. That strength is mostly employed in violent circles — police work, dubious activities. **Duelists are badly regarded by default.**
