1. Portrait duel field strategy

Below 1024px the duel must render vertically. The board is an 8-column pixel geometry with a horizontal hand band, a side rail and geometry-anchored phase controls.

2. Portrait deck editor strategy

The editor is a three-panel desktop layout (catalog · deck workspace · card details) with drag and drop; portrait has room for one panel.

3. Does the restyle change duel field visuals that carry locked acceptance evidence?

The duel field's colors and geometry are pinned by ADR-015 halo semantics, the DF-16 Chromium parity gate and a six-case acceptance matrix that just went green on the branch you are merging first.

4. Where does the visual language come from?

Three palettes exist today: duel dark blue, VN prototype (with authored direction boards), deck editor slate. One has to win.

5. Does the deck picker list local decks for story and standalone duels?

Story duels reuse the deck picker, and the plan dispatches local decks to the Worker — so the picker becomes the place where bundled and local decks meet.

6. What happens on reload during a story duel?

The Worker dies on reload, so an in-progress duel cannot resume. The route #/duel/session/:handoffId will be sitting in the address bar.

7. Worktree to branch mapping and integration ownership

Three named branches exist, but shell, routes, tokens, contracts and config need a single owner or three lanes will fight over them.

8. How much of the restructure lands before the three lanes fork?

Parallel work only pays off once the shared surface is stable; forking too early means three lanes rebasing on every shell change.
