# Explanation from reviewer B for candidate comprehension

## 1. Outcome and scope

Improve one required `ai-bot` through reproducible paired games against frozen opponents, including its starting AI. Optional inputs are extra opponents, owner limits and a compatible checkpoint. Success delivers accepted source, verified results and compact history; no qualifying change is an honest unchanged outcome. Only target AI changes. Decks, engine, legality and hidden-information boundaries remain protected. This explanation describes the candidate package without executing it.

## 2. Process overview

Prepare freezes configuration and calibrates two pools; Evaluate establishes baseline or measures a candidate; Decide records acceptance; Analyze always diagnoses that evaluation. Replay tests isolated hypotheses, Assemble combines qualifying edits, and evaluation repeats. The decision ceiling or no qualifying edit leads to Deliver. Incidents can suspend any active stage. The stateless controller prints legal tasks/edges; owning project tools persist state, while the orchestrator verifies semantic gates.

## 8. Ambiguities and interpretations

| ID | Instruction reference | Ambiguity | Chosen interpretation | Behavioral consequence | Clarification required? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Autonomy / required limits | Defaults exist alongside asking for missing required limits | Resolve supplied defaults first; ask only when necessary schema limits remain unresolved | Avoid unnecessary approval stops | Only if unresolved |
| A2 | Replay conversion gate | Five conversions and 5% denominator | Require both globally per reason, with ignored timeouts retained in associated-loss denominator | A timeout never converts; unchanged/nonwinning choices merely fail conversion | No |

Scoring uses equal opponent weights, draws as zero wins in the denominator, and completed paired intersections excluding either side's ignored game. Every opponent requires a completed pair; unresolved comparability is unassessable. No confidence intervals justify promotion. Analysts see target-only completed losses, never adjacent or two-perspective gameplay. Push, publication and merge require separate authorization.
