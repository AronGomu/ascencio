# Gameplay structures

## Story loop

> Story Event → Map / Freedom → Optional Activity → Next Story Event

The player is not pushed continuously from cutscene to cutscene. Major story events are associated with duels.

## Duel categories

### Story Duel

- Usually Best of 3.
- Must be won to progress.
- Used for major story confrontations.

### Narrative Duel

- Single game or Best of 3.
- Can be won or lost.
- Story continues either way.
- Dialogue changes based on result.

### Free Duel

- Repeatable.
- Provides points/resources and deck testing.
- Does not determine main story progression.

## No forced playable losses

The story must not require the player to canonically lose a duel the player actively controls. A required loss belongs in a non-player-controlled narrative event.

## Map and freedom mode

The full academy map opens immediately after the Qualification Arc. Content unlocks over time rather than map regions:

- Free Duel opponents.
- Story events.
- Shops.
- Future side quests.
- Future social interactions.
- Future side modes.

The player initially has exploration but little repeatable content.

## Free Duel introduction

The Nerd Arc introduces repeatable duels. Fynn first faces three classmates or bullies in the main story. After that sequence, they become repeatable map opponents. This makes the system tutorial diegetic.

## Deferred modes

These remain post-main-story features:

- Roguelike duel mode.
- Social or relationship system.
- Side quests.
- Outfit and cosmetic unlocks.
- Additional side activities.

The architecture should support later addition without making these current production priorities.

## Future roguelike mode

The player starts with any owned deck and 8,000 persistent Life Points. Duels chain together, remaining LP carries forward, and each victory grants a cumulative passive bonus. The objective is to survive as many opponents as possible. A simulator or training room may host this mode later.
