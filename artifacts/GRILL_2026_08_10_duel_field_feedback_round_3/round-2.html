1. How and when do the four .ydk files arrive?

You are authoring them, and the deck epic is now ticket 1 — so this is the plan's only hard blocker. The files land at src/duel/presets/decks/*.ydk and are imported with Vite's ?raw, so they are pure data: nothing in the code depends on their contents.

2. What shape does the deck picker take, and which decks does it list?

The app auto-starts a duel the moment the worker reports ready (duel-store.ts calls client.startDuel(MVP_PRESET_ID) against one hardcoded preset). A picker means that auto-start goes away or changes owner, and "Play again" has to decide whether it re-picks.

3. What exactly is the collapsed preview, and the narrow fallback?

You kept the 44 px floor and said the preview yields. So it degrades twice: on short viewports it becomes "a thin strip", and on narrow ones it moves to a full-width bottom band. Both need a definition precise enough to test.

4. Draggable windows: does outside-click still close them, and do they share a position?

Item 4 asks that clicking outside the zone list closes it like the close button. Your round 1 precision then made that same dialog a floating draggable window, and window-like things usually do not vanish when you click away. The confirm window (item 30) has the same tension.

5. What goes into localStorage, now that it exists?

Nothing in src/ touches localStorage today. Item 30 introduces it, and once it exists, "remember my deck choice" and "remember auto-place" become one-line additions or deliberate omissions. Deciding the schema once avoids four incompatible ones.

6. Confirm the reveal rule table for "face-down but known"

You chose proper worker-side tracking. This is the exact table I would implement; it must be conservative by construction — the projection may forget a reveal, it must never invent one, or the client leaks hidden information.

7. What about the opponent policy, once it is holding a Nekroz deck?

OpponentPolicy.ts is a first-legal heuristic: summon the first legal monster, activate the first legal effect, attack with the strongest. Against 22 vanilla cards that is fine. Handed Shaddoll or Nekroz it will make nonsense plays and, more importantly, it can answer a prompt in a way that produces the same prompt again.

8. Item 17 — how big is the hover zoom, and where does it apply?

"Add an animation and zoom to make the card bigger… and ensure the halo follows the card's size." On a board where a card is ~70 px wide, the multiplier decides whether this reads as polish or as a card obscuring three of its neighbours.

9. Item 29 — does a single candidate still need confirming?

Item 29 says: with one card, clicking it opens a dialog to confirm. Item 10 says: do not ask to confirm once you click an attack target. Both are selections with exactly one legal answer, so they need one rule, not two.

10. What proves a deck "works"?

The deck epic is ticket 1, so everything after it inherits whatever bar we set here. The duel seed is random per run, so a single passing run proves very little — round 2's handoff is explicit about that.
