# Global scenario

> Status: working draft

## Core concept

Ascensio is a 100% original Yu-Gi-Oh! fan game set in a parallel universe with original characters and no connection to the official Yu-Gi-Oh! universe.

The game takes inspiration from older Yu-Gi-Oh! games such as *Spirit Caller*, *The Duelists of the Roses*, *Nightmare Troubadour* and the *World Championship* games.

## Era structure

Each chapter broadly corresponds to one Yu-Gi-Oh! era and introduces its signature mechanic.

| Chapter | Era | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Duel Monsters | Fusion and Ritual |
| 2 | GX | None currently planned |
| 3 | 5D's | Synchro |
| 4 | ZEXAL | Xyz |
| 5 | ARC-V | Pendulum |
| 6 | VRAINS | Link |

Each chapter should work as a standalone game while increasing story depth and mechanic complexity.

## World foundation

Dueling is one of society's normal conflict-resolution systems. People use duels to settle claims, negotiate stakes, enforce commitments and resolve confrontations. Characters generally treat this as obvious and do not explain it in dialogue.

Anyone may challenge another person if both sides place a meaningful stake. Stakes may include cards, property, money, information, services, commitments, privileges, access or another meaningful concession. The older requirement for incompatible ownership claims is removed.

Physical violence cannot normally acquire things or force outcomes. Duel training therefore supports practical careers comparable to police, security, military-style conflict specialists, investigators, enforcement and protection roles. Other duelists pursue tournaments, spectacle, entertainment and showmanship.

## Academy status

Duel Academy uses three student classes:

| Class | Uniform | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Red | Lowest-ranked duelists |
| Yellow | Yellow | Intermediate duelists |
| Blue | Blue | Highest-ranked duelists |

Class depends mainly on demonstrated dueling level and academy status, not school year. Students in one year can belong to different classes.

## Duel rules

A duel engaged over a conflict cannot be refused. Refusal is an automatic loss and cedes the stake. Several duelists may jointly engage one person; the rules do not balance this imbalance. A person's physical integrity cannot be staked, except in a Shadow Game where the stake is the soul.

Cheating is not forbidden by the rules. It is sanctioned only when discovered and proven through a binding logical argument; the cheater then automatically loses the duel and stake. A failed accusation has no formal sanction, but costs time, reveals suspicion and may damage reputation. This is a world rule, not Chapter 1's plot or thesis.

## Card spirits and scarcity

A spirit world exists. Some spirits imprint energy into manufactured cards through successive print runs. Not every card is imprinted, creating scarcity, a market and covetousness. The exact supernatural system remains open.

## Philosophical foundation

The story uses an objectivist foundation. The hero does not begin virtuous; he progresses through lessons built into the story. Dueling expresses character, preparation and rational choice rather than fate.

Magic is hard magic: coherent, knowable laws with explicit costs and limits. Supernatural phenomena are natural phenomena inside the fiction and can be studied through reason. The supernatural layer becomes visible during Chapter 1 while its exact rules remain open.

The first three chapters form a moral progression:

| Chapter | Fynn's position | Error |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | He treats impulse as self-interest | Whim-worship and lack of discipline |
| 2 | He rejects impulse through self-sacrifice | Destructive altruism |
| 3 | He integrates self-interest with reason | Rational egoism |
