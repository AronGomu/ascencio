# Character creation grid

> Status: reference method — stable

The reference grid used for this project. It crosses Lajos Egri (*The Art of Dramatic Writing*), John Truby (*The Anatomy of Story*), Robert McKee (*Story*) and the Randian approach to a character as the embodiment of a premise.

**1. Bone structure (Egri)**

- Physiology: age, body, health, what one notices first, what he does with his appearance
- Sociology: origin, family, money, status at the academy, education received, who formed him
- Psychology: temperament, kind of intelligence, relation to authority, what makes him angry, what shames him

**2. Inner engine (Truby)**

- Moral weakness: how he harms others without seeing it
- Psychological weakness: what harms himself
- Need: what he has to understand in order to stop being what he is
- Desire: the concrete, visible objective of the chapter
- Ghost: the past event that produced the weakness
- Revelation: what, and at exactly which moment

**3. Premise (Egri + Rand)**

- Which idea does he embody? Stated as a causal sentence: "X leads to Y"
- His answer to: *what makes a life successful?*
- What does he ground his judgments on: reason, other people's opinions, emotions, tradition?
- What does he do when his values conflict with his comfort?
- **First-hander or second-hander?** (the central axis of this story)

**4. Pressure (McKee)**

- Which impossible choice can break him, and which one will reveal him?
- What he says he wants vs. what he actually wants
- The gap between the image he projects and what he is
- What would he lose by winning what he desires?

**5. Yu-Gi-Oh! layer**

- What his deck says about him: archetype, strategy, tempo (aggro / control / combo = three psychologies)
- How does he cheat, or why does he refuse to?
- Does a spirit inhabit his cards? Does he know it? What relationship does he have with it?
- What is he prepared to stake, and where does he stop?
- How does he react to a multi-against-one duel — does he accept it, organise it, flee it?
- What does his way of **losing** reveal?

**Coherence test:** the answers of layer 5 have to follow from the answer of layer 3. If the deck does not say the same thing as the philosophy, one of the two is badly chosen.
