1. What does round 3 branch off?

Round 2 is complete and pushed but never merged; every one of your 30 items describes that branch's build, so basing on main would make the plan unimplementable.

2. Is item 12 — four archetype decks plus a pre-duel deck picker — part of this plan?

Item 12 is not a UI item. It touches the deck files, the reviewed card-pool gate, the Vite image manifest, the pre-duel flow, and it exposes the engine to Xyz/Ritual/Fusion/Pendulum summoning that the prompt UI has never once been driven through. On its own it is roughly the size of the other 29 items combined.

3. Who authors the four decklists?

"Burning Abyss from Duelist Alliance", "Necroz at its first extension release", "Shaddoll of Duelist Alliance", "Spellbook around the Duelist Alliance metagame" each have several defensible 2014–2015 builds, and the exact 40+15 matters for whether the duel is playable.

4. What happens to the reviewed card-pool gate?

src/duel/presets/deck-parser.ts hard-codes 22 approved card codes and validateDeck throws for anything outside them. Four archetype decks cannot load until this changes, and the gate exists deliberately to stop unreviewed effects entering the MVP.

5. Item 9 — when the view cannot fit 100 % of the viewport height, what gives?

Round 1 pinned a rule that every field target stays ≥44 px, which forces .duel-field-board { min-width: 52rem } and a 16:9 box. At 1366×768 that board is 932×524; add the header and a bottom preview panel and the column needs ~800 px against 768. One of the two constraints has to yield, and this is the single decision the most items depend on.

6. Items 20 and 21 — confirm the final preview placement

Item 20 says give the unused left space to the preview; item 21 says there is not enough room beside the field at all, so move it to the bottom with the art on the left and the text on the right. They describe different layouts and 21 reads as the later thought.

7. Items 6, 7, 13, 15 — the hand band, all four at once

Four items touch the hand and they pull in different directions: 6 deletes the hand zone rectangle, 13 says the hand should span exactly the five spell/trap zones, 15 says reduce the hand width so the GY/banished/deck column can attach to the fifth monster and spell/trap zones, and 7 caps it at 10 cards with arrows and a scrollbar.

8. Does the opponent's hand get the same treatment?

Item 6 and 7 are written from your side. The opponent's hand is a row of card backs in its own zone, and item 26 separately asks to delete the badge that currently sits at that position.

9. Item 23 — what decides that the Extra Monster Zones get removed?

"If none of the players have any Link monster, remove those zones." Your own extra deck is knowable; your opponent's extra deck is, strictly, hidden information — reading it to decide a layout leaks one bit ("they run Links").

10. Item 25 — confirm the whole halo palette, not just the two states you named

You set green = valid target and orange = selected/awaiting confirmation, and said it overrides earlier claims. Four other halo states already exist in the CSS and need a colour that does not collide: drop candidate (item 18 asks for green), keyboard-navigation focus, the animation "feedback target", and the hover state item 29 wants on list entries.

11. Items 18 and 19 — how far do the drag physics go?

You asked for the feasibility read before committing. Here it is: no library is needed and none should be added. A dragged card is one absolutely-positioned element; the whole effect is a transform string recomputed in a requestAnimationFrame loop that already runs only while a pointer is down. Cost below is honest implementation size, and every tier respects prefers-reduced-motion.

12. Item 30 — where does the floating confirm window remember its position?

"Save its last position so that it reappears at the same spot." Round 2 settled that UI settings stay in memory for the session with no persistence, so this needs an explicit ruling rather than an inherited one.

13. Items 28 and 29 — does the list dialog also replace on-field targeting?

Your example is Monster Reborn, whose targets all sit in a graveyard. Many effects target cards on the board instead, or a mix of both — and the field already highlights those in place.

14. Item 1 — how far does "face-down but I know what it is" go?

Your example — a monster your opponent Special Summoned face-down out of their graveyard — is genuinely public knowledge, and the projector currently strips its code. Making it previewable means the worker has to remember which face-down cards were publicly revealed, the same machinery round 2 built for deck reveals.

15. Item 8 — what text goes next to the life points?

"Add the name or role of the player… indicating whether it's you or your opponent." What that string should be depends on whether the deck picker from item 12 exists yet.
