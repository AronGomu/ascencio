# Philosophical foundation

> Status: working draft — Chapter 1 in design

The story is founded on **objectivist philosophy** (Ayn Rand), present at every level: aesthetics, values, structure of the conflicts.

## Guiding principles

- The hero does not start out virtuous. He progresses toward virtue across the chapters, through lessons built into the story.
- The duel is an expression of the duelist's character and rational choice — not a clash of brute power, and not an intervention of fate.
- **Magic is hard magic**: fixed, coherent, knowable laws, with explicit costs and limits. This is not mysticism in the Randian sense, since mysticism is the claim to know *without* reason or proof. Here magic is one more natural phenomenon, studiable by reason.
- Exploitable corollary: in this world, whoever treats magic as an unfathomable mystery is **wrong within the fiction itself**; whoever studies and masters it is right. The epistemological conflict is a narrative engine.

## Central thesis of Chapter 1

Cheating does not pay — neither morally nor practically. Fraud harms the one who commits it.

## The moral trilogy

The arc that spans chapters 1 to 3.

| Chapter | Fynn's position | Error |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | He believes he is an egoist | He is only impulsive (whim-worship) |
| 2 | He rejects impulsiveness | He tips into sacrificial altruism and destroys himself |
| 3 | He discovers the third term | Rational egoism — neither of the two |
